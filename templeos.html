The operating system

TempleOS

A complete operating system, written largely by one man, built around one language and one fixed display mode.

What it was

TempleOS is a 64-bit operating system for PCs. Terry A. Davis wrote most of it, largely on his own, mostly in HolyC: the kernel, the shell, the editor, the compiler, the games and the documents. It is deliberately unlike other systems: one display mode, one language, one address space and, by most accounts, no networking.

Davis described it as a temple for God, and said he built it because God told him to. That belief shaped the design, and it is part of why the project is remembered. This site records his claim as his claim, and concentrates on the software.

The design, in facts

    TempleOS was created by Terry A. Davis, who wrote most of it largely on his own.

    The sole-author claim and its scale are repeated in nearly every account.

  • Firm

  • Reported

    Often cited as a deliberate design choice.

  • Reported

  • Reported

  • Reported

The screen

The display is 640 by 480 pixels with 16 colours, and that is all. The 16 colours are the standard VGA palette, in the order and with the names TempleOS uses for them, as recalled; the hex values are the standard VGA values.

  1. 0 BLACK
  2. 1 BLUE
  3. 2 GREEN
  4. 3 CYAN
  5. 4 RED
  6. 5 PURPLE
  7. 6 BROWN
  8. 7 LTGRAY

  14. 13 LTPURPLE
  15. 14 YELLOW
  16. 15 WHITE

Running it today

TempleOS is usually run inside a virtual machine, a program that pretends to be a PC, such as QEMU, VirtualBox or VMware. Because it has no network stack and runs everything at the highest privilege, it is best treated as a curiosity to explore in a sandbox, not as a system to use. Reported

Several community projects continue the language or the system. The resources page lists a few.
